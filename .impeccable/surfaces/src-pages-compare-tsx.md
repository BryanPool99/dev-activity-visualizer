# Surface brief — El duelo

## Scope and visitor mode

One route: `/compare?a={user}&b={user}`. Mode **Operate**, with the one Persuade beat the product has: the moment two rhythms are set against each other. This surface lives inside the world the report established; it adds an instrument, not a new identity.

## Audience, job, action

The visitor who has already seen a report and now wants to put two people — or two versions of themselves — next to each other. Job: find the difference in how they work, not in how much. Action: type two usernames, press Compare.

## Proof and content

Every number on the page is a number that also appears in that person's own report, matched by date and by hour. The duel adds no metric of its own; it puts the existing ones on a shared surface.

## Chosen direction

The canon, inherited whole: light neutral, hairline cards, one sans, one accent. The duel earns the accent a second job — it *is* the second account, so the two sides are always distinguishable by hue alone.

## Memorable moment

The clock with two bands and the marked shared hours: the reading a single profile page cannot give, and the reason to come back with a second name.

## Direction contract

**THESIS** — The duel is not two reports side by side; it is one instrument holding two rhythms. It refuses the arrangement every comparison tool ships — two columns of statistics the reader has to measure against each other — and replaces it with a single shared surface: one grid where each day belongs to whoever pushed more, and one clock carrying both hours at once.

**OWN-WORLD** — The same neutral scale and one accent as the report, with the accent doing a second job: it is the second account. The first account is ink `#111827`, the second the accent `#2563EB`, a tie is `#d1d5db`, an empty day `#f3f4f6`. Everything else is the canon — hairline cards, one sans at three weights, sentence-case headings, no shadows, no gradients, no radius above 8px. The clock is two concentric bands, the inner one the first account and the outer one the second, so one glance reads both rhythms and the hours they meet on.

**STORY** — The visitor arrives with two names and leaves with a sentence they can repeat: they work in the morning, she works at night, they overlap at two in the afternoon. The grid says who led each day, the clock says when, and the verdict list says who takes each measure. Nothing here is a new claim; it is the two reports, read against each other.

**FIRST VIEWPORT** — 1440×900. The header is unchanged from the report. A 24px page gutter. 24px below, the two identities side by side with a "vs" between them: a 40px avatar, the name in 14/600, and the account's total in 12/400 muted. 24px below that, the duel card runs full width: its header row reads "Day by day, side by side" 14/600 on the left and the count of shared days on the right; under it the grid, left-aligned, with a three-colour legend beneath. The grid's lower edge lands near y=470, so the dial card and the verdict card are already in view.

**FORM** — Canon, the same world as the report. The duel is a surface inside an established world: the world stays fixed and only the task, the content and the instrument change.

**FINISH** — unreviewed and undocumented is unfinished; this build ends with the finish review, the verdict, DESIGN.md, and every shipping raster carrying its provenance
